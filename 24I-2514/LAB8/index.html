Lab Task 1: Richest Countries (1952 vs 2007)

Step 1: Predict first
"Which country is the richest in 2007? Is it the same in 1952?"
In 2007, Norway is the richest country (GDP per capita ~$49,357). In 1952, it was not the same: Kuwait was the richest country by a huge margin (~$108,382), while Norway was 6th (~$10,095).

Step 6: Explain it back (one sentence each)

  1. Which line keeps only the top 10?
    The line .slice(0, 10) keeps only the first 10 items after sorting descending with .sort((a, b) => b.gdpPercap - a.gdpPercap).
  2. Why do we write d => d.country inside .data(...)?
    It is a key function for object constancy, so D3 connects each bar to the country name instead of the index, making the bars slide smoothly into their new positions.
  3. In 1952, why do the other bars look so short?
    Kuwait had an extremely high GDP per capita ($108,382), which stretched the linear scale so much that all other countries (under $15,000) look very small in comparison.

Answer in one sentence each:

  1. How many <rect> did you expect? How many did you get? Why?
    We expected 142 <rect> elements, but got 1,704 because the CSV has 12 years of data for 142 countries (142 × 12 = 1,704 rows) and we did not filter by year.
  2. How tall is one bar? Can you read the names?
    One bar is only about 2 to 3 pixels tall, and the 142 country names overlap into an unreadable black smudge.
  3. After your first fix, is the chart easy to read? What would you change?
    Even after filtering to a single year (142 bars), it is still too cramped to read, so we should filter to only the top 10 richest countries.
  4. Now show the 10 richest countries. Is Norway in your chart? If not, what is missing?
    Norway is #1 in 2007; if it was missing, filtering specifically by year === 2007 before sorting was missing.
